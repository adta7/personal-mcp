Pasting one prompt into three models and feeding each answer back to the others catches real
mistakes, but it is slow and easy to do inconsistently. This automates that loop: independent
drafts in parallel, cross-critique grounded in the original prompt, an explicit extraction of
what every draft missed, and a synthesis step that is instructed to *choose and cut* rather
than average.

The design decision that matters is the grounding rule. Models asked to critique each other
tend to converge on style. Anchoring every critique to the original prompt keeps the question
"does this answer what was asked" rather than "would I have written it this way."

Every run is saved as structured JSON and readable Markdown, so the reasoning trail can be
inspected afterward rather than trusted at the moment it scrolls past.

This site's own architecture plan was reviewed by a version of this tool. The verdict was
*approve with major changes*, and the changes were substantial — the strongest finding was an
attack I had underweighted, and the review reversed one design argument I had defended.
