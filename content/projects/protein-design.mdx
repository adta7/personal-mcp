An orchestration layer over existing structure-prediction, molecular dynamics, and docking
models — deliberately not an attempt to reimplement AlphaFold. It fetches and measures a
structure, folds a sequence and grades it against field-standard metrics, prepares a
simulatable system, runs dynamics, redocks a ligand, and scores a protein–protein interface
with DockQ, all drivable from a desktop workbench.

**Scope honesty first:** the pipeline produces *educational-grade* results. Simplified force
fields, picosecond trajectories, hosted single models. It teaches the correct workflow and
how to check it — not publishable numbers.

That caveat is about the outputs. It is not about the following, which are findings about
*evaluation* and do not depend on force-field quality.

## What measuring it taught me

**A date-based held-out test set is mostly not held out.** The standard protection against
memorization is to test on structures released after a model's training cutoff. Measured
against the PDB, 87% of post-cutoff entries have a ≥70%-identical pre-cutoff relative. Only
**6.3% are genuinely unseen.** Most "held-out" evaluation is measuring interpolation.

**An uncertain prediction is a distribution, not a structure.** Five identical requests for
one complex returned DockQ scores from 0.030 to 0.501 — spanning three CAPRI quality classes.
The model's own confidence ranking selected the *worst* of the five. Reporting a single
structure from a stochastic sampler, with a confidence score used to pick it, can be actively
misleading.

**I was wrong about what was missing, and the ablation said so.** Nine of fourteen complexes
failed outright, and the cause turned out to be the pipeline rather than the model: adding a
per-chain MSA recovered five, four of them to CAPRI `high` (one moved 0.079 → 0.927). I had
assumed *paired* MSAs were the missing capability. An ablation recovered the identical five
targets **without** pairing, reached a higher class, and was 14× more stable.

**Most of the benchmark's "model failures" were my own target selection.** Five of fourteen
complexes were unanswerable as specified — two covalent conjugates, a molecular-glue ternary
complex, a disulfide-stapled pair, a homodimer. Audited down to well-posed targets, 9 of 9
succeed. And one remaining ceiling turned out to be a missing input rather than a hard
problem: one chain of 9CU8 has no retrievable homolog at all, caught by an adequacy gate
*before* folding, so its score was never a measurement of difficulty.

## How it is built

Every phase is pre-registered: a freeze script resolves every sequence, hash, threshold, and
decision rule into a validated JSON manifest **before** an API call is spent, and the runner
refuses to proceed on a hash mismatch. Decisions live in 37 ADRs. Two planned features were
costed and cancelled by arithmetic rather than built.
